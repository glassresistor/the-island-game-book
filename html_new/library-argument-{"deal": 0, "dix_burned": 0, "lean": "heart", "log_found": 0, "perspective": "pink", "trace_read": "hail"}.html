We're in one of the social alcoves off the ventral notochord, taking a break from the library. She has decided to start again from first principles. I eye her with an understandable mix of confusion and mistrust; my claim is almost too stupid to deny.

"Eriophora's impossible, you know. Violates the laws of physics."

"It's true," she assures me. "Takes way too much energy to accelerate a ship with Eri's mass, especially at relativistic speeds. You'd need the energy output of a whole sun. People figured if we made it to the stars at all, we'd have to do it ships maybe the size of your thumb. Crew them with virtual personalities downloaded onto chips."

That's too nonsensical even for me. "Wrong. Don't have mass, can't fall towards anything. Eri wouldn't even work if it was that small."

"But suppose you can't displace any of that mass. No wormholes, no Higgs conduits, nothing to throw your gravitational field in the direction of travel. Your center of mass just sits there in, well, the center of your mass."

A spastic head-shake of mine. "Do have those things!"

"Sure we do. But for the longest time, we didn't know it."

My foot taps an agitated tattoo on the deck.

"It's the history of the species," she explains. Her hands are hurting. She looks down, surprised: her fists are clenched so tightly that her nails cut into the flesh of her palms. It takes a real effort to open them again.

I watch her do it from across the alcove, and something in my chest — some old, untrained part of me that the chimp never bothered to calibrate — hurts in the same place where her hands are hurting.

She almost remembers how this feels. She used to feel this way all the time. Way back when everything mattered; before passion faded to ritual, before rage cooled to disdain. Before Sunday Ahzmundin, eternity's warrior, settled for heaping insults on stunted children.

Parts of this ship are still scorched and uninhabitable, even now. Whatever she was back then, whatever they did to each other — it left scars on the bones of this ship, and I can feel them in the deck under my feet every time I walk past.

She is awake, and she is alone, and she is sick of being outnumbered by morons. There are rules and there are risks and you don't wake the dead on a whim, but fuck it. She's calling reinforcements.
