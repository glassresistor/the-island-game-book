She wakes first. I know this because the chimp tells me to be ready, and then it tells her that I'm here, and then she is sitting up in her coffin with her eyes open and looking at me like I am something that fell out of the sky.

"Who are you?" she whispers. Her voice is a ruin. She hasn't used it in millennia.

"I'm yours," I say.

It comes out wrong. It's not what I was supposed to say — the chimp gave me a script, and the script says You weren't scheduled, but Chimp wants extra hands on deck. Next build's got a situation. But my mouth does what it always does when I'm scared: it tells the truth instead of the line.

And just like that she is a mother. I see it happen in her face. It takes me a moment to understand that this is not a kindness. It is a trap with my name on it, and I am already inside it.

"You weren't scheduled," I finish, because the script still has to come out somewhere. "But Chimp wants extra hands on deck. Next build's got a situation."

"Situation?" she asks.

"Contact scenario, maybe."

She studies my face for a long time. I don't know what she is looking for. Later I will learn that she can see the damage in me — the tiny flipped base-pairs, the way I grew up wrong on this ship — and that it tells her something about how far out we are. I can feel her reading me like a data sheet.

"Sun up ahead," I say. "Half lightyear. Chimp thinks — maybe it's talking to us. Anyhow..." I shrug. It's the only gesture I know that means I don't know either. "No rush. Lotsa time."

She nods, but she is waiting for something. The Question, I will learn later. Everyone on this ship waits for the same question after a thaw: How long? How long have we been dead?

I don't ask it. It's not my question to ask. After all this time, I don't want to know either.
