I refuse to choose for the machine, and I refuse to choose for myself, which turns out to be the same refusal.

I burn out my own link — the last prying eye the chimp ever had into a head it didn't own — and I go down into the crypt and I do not come back up. Not for this build. Not for the next one either, if I can help it. I ration what's left of my candle to the barest minimum: just enough to watch the gate boot behind me from the dark, just enough to feel the scorching wavefront chase me into the void and pass, as it has a thousand times before.

Up there, the mission goes on without me. The Island is saved or it isn't; my son grows up or he doesn't; the chimp finds another way, because the chimp always finds another way. It doesn't matter. I have learned the only thing worth learning out here: that I can watch any point in time, or a hundred thousand, if I slice my life thinly enough — but I will never see everything, and the wanting is not a bug to be engineered out. It's the whole point of being the small, warm, finite thing at the center of all that endless cold.

The chimp's dead black eye gazes down from the ceiling of my coffin, carbon-scoring and all. I am reluctant to venture out where its nerves have not been so thoroughly cauterized. But I stay in here. For once, being blind is exactly what I wanted.

The end.
