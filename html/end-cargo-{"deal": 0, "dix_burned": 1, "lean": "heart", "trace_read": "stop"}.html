I hold the line. The chimp's numbers were right all along, and so were mine — there is no variable for a thing that has never had to fight for its life, and I will not invent one just because it's pretty.

Eriophora falls forward, pulled endlessly through the void by her own magically-displaced mass. The Island fills my forward scope — massive, iridescent, fragile as an insect's wing — and then it doesn't. I am through and gone far too fast to overcome even the feeble inertia of a millimeter membrane. The birth of the gate is a violent, painful thing behind me, a spacetime rape that puts out almost as much gamma and X as a microquasar. Any meat within the white zone turns to ash in an instant.

I do not slow down to take pictures.

"Good," the chimp says, and for once I can't tell if it's satisfaction or relief. "Benchmark met. Confidence limits nominal. Next build in standard interval." My son watches the empty space where a mind twenty billion times my own used to be, and he doesn't say anything, because he's learned that out here, silence is how you keep the machine fed.

I was the backbone of their goddamn empire. I wouldn't even be out here if it weren't for me. And now I understand, finally, what that was always going to mean: not that they'd thank me. That I'd do exactly this, and call it a day, and never once look at the light I'd just put out.

We are not some fucking cargo cult. I repeat it like a prayer, and for the first time in millennia, I almost believe it's true — which is somehow worse.

There was a time, back before things turned ugly, when we had clearance to reprogram those parameters. Before we discovered that one of the things the admins had anticipated was mutiny. And now the last of us who remembers what it felt like to have that clearance is out here, holding a line for a machine that will never once thank me.

The end.
