You hold the line. The chimp's numbers were right all along, and so were yours — there is no variable for a thing that has never had to fight for its life, and you will not invent one just because it's pretty.

Eriophora falls forward, pulled endlessly through the void by her own magically-displaced mass. The Island fills your forward scope — massive, iridescent, fragile as an insect's wing — and then it doesn't. You are through and gone far too fast to overcome even the feeble inertia of a millimeter membrane. The birth of the gate is a violent, painful thing behind you, a spacetime rape that puts out almost as much gamma and X as a microquasar. Any meat within the white zone turns to ash in an instant.

You do not slow down to take pictures. One of the reasons, anyway.

"Good," the chimp says, and for once you can't tell if it's satisfaction or relief. "Benchmark met. Confidence limits nominal. Next build in standard interval." Your son watches the empty space where a mind twenty billion times your own used to be, and he doesn't say anything, because he's learned that out here, saying nothing is how you keep the machine fed.

You were the backbone of their goddamn empire. You wouldn't even be out here if it weren't for you. And now you understand, finally, what that was always going to mean: not that they'd thank you. That you'd do exactly this, and call it a day, and never once look at the light you'd just put out.

We are not some fucking cargo cult. You repeat it like a prayer, and for the first time in millennia, you almost believe it's true — which is somehow worse.

The end.
