You save it — and this time you stay long enough to try, because you have finally decided that a road built for people who never look back is not worth the building, and you would rather spend what's left of your candle on the one thing in the void that looked back.

It takes most of your remaining life to build a pidgin that could hold the truths and philosophies of a mind vaster than all humanity. And it works — or it feels like it works, which out here is the same thing, where there is no one to tell you the difference. You learn to read the gap between a blink and a sentence. You stop translating every flicker as stop. One day, in the space between two deceleration curves, you are almost — almost — certain it says something new. Something with a shape that "stop" never had. Your hands shake when you log it. For the first time since the gates went dark, you are not building a road. You are sitting beside something that is trying to tell you a secret.

You go down to the crypt to conserve what's left of your life for the next build, and in the dark — no instruments, no tank, nothing but the pattern still running behind your own eyes — your mind keeps working it the way it always works things when there is nothing else to do. And that is when it stops feeling like a secret.

The new signal has the same spacing as the old one. The same log-linear cycle. The same 92.5 corsecs. You decoded this pattern years ago; you wrote the decode in your own head and never once doubted it. There is nothing new here. There has never been anything new here. Your finite, lonely brain — a small warm thing rationing itself out across an endless cold — took a pattern it already understood and, because it so desperately wanted to be spoken to, manufactured a meaning out of the noise and called it communion. You were not sitting beside something trying to tell you a secret. You were sitting beside yourself, in the dark, talking.

And you'd read it as a hail, back at the tank — something out there trying to get your attention. You were wrong about who was calling, but right about the shape of the need: someone was reaching out into the dark, and it turned out the only one who would ever answer was you.

Angels do not speak to ants. That was never the hard part. The hard part is that they don't even know the ants are there.

You wake for the next build. First thing in the tank: DHF428, flickering — steady as a standard candle on the right, stuttering on the left, the way it always does. Stop. Stop. Stop. And you look at it with the calm of someone who has stopped hoping, and you know — you have always known, in the part of you that keeps the tally — that you will do this again. Save it. Burn your life. Almost believe. Every build is a little dream about being the one where it finally speaks, and every build ends in the same cold coffin, under the same carbon-scoring, with the same pattern running behind your eyes, waiting for you to be lonely enough to give it a name.

The end.
