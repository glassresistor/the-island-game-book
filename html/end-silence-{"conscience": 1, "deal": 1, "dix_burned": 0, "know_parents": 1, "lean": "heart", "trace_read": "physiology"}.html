You refuse to choose for the machine, and you refuse to choose for yourself, which turns out to be the same refusal.

You burn out your own link — the last prying eye the chimp ever had into a head it didn't own — and you go down into the crypt and you do not come back up. Not for this build. Not for the next one either, if you can help it. You ration what's left of your candle to the barest minimum: just enough to watch the gate boot behind you from the dark, just enough to feel the scorching wavefront chase you into the void and pass, as it has a thousand times before.

Up there, the mission goes on without you. The Island is saved or it isn't; your son grows up or he doesn't; the chimp finds another way, because the chimp always finds another way. It doesn't matter. You have learned the only thing worth learning out here: that you can watch any point in time, or a hundred thousand, if you slice your life thinly enough — but you will never see everything, and the wanting is not a bug to be engineered out. It's the whole point of being the small, warm, finite thing at the center of all that endless cold.

The chimp's dead black eye gazes down from the ceiling of your coffin, carbon-scoring and all. You are reluctant to venture out where its nerves have not been so thoroughly cauterised. But you stay in here. For once, being blind is exactly what you wanted.

The end.
