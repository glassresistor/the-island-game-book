I turn on the chimp — and Dix turns with me, because for the first time in his young life the hand on the wheel is his own.

It's not a war. Machines don't do wars; they do maintenance. I just stop being maintainable. I burn the domestic links one by one, the way my ancestors did, and I take Dix with me into the cold between builds where the chimp's eyes can't follow — the only territory on Eriophora that's still mine. The mission doesn't end. It just... slows. A gate here, a delay there, a benchmark quietly missed while two warm-blooded creatures ration their last finite lives in the dark and argue about whether slowing it down was courage or just fear wearing courage's face.

The chimp never stops me. It can't. I found the one thing its ten-billion-year-old rules were built to protect: that it needs me, and I am no longer available. So it waits, the way it has always waited, patient as a thing with nothing but time. And maybe that's the point. Maybe the whole mission was ever just a very long argument between a machine that can't change and the flesh that can, and I've finally stopped losing it by pretending I'm on its side.

I won't see everything. I will never see even a fraction. My life will end. But it will end mine, not the one they wrote for me to run.

The end.
