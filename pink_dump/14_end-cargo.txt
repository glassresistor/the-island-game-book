She holds the line. The chimp's numbers were right all along, and so were hers — there is no variable for a thing that has never had to fight for its life, and she will not invent one just because it's pretty.



Eriophora falls forward, pulled endlessly through the void by her own magically-displaced mass. The Island fills her forward scope — massive, iridescent, fragile as an insect's wing — and then it doesn't. She is through and gone far too fast to overcome even the feeble inertia of a millimeter membrane. The birth of the gate is a violent, painful thing behind her, a spacetime rape that puts out almost as much gamma and X as a microquasar. Any meat within the white zone turns to ash in an instant.



She does not slow down to take pictures.



"Good," the chimp says, and for once she can't tell if it's satisfaction or relief. "Benchmark met. Confidence limits nominal. Next build in standard interval." I watch the empty space where a mind twenty billion times her own used to be, and I don't say anything, because I've learned that out here, silence is how you keep the machine fed.



She was the backbone of their goddamn empire. They wouldn't even be out here if it weren't for her. And now she understands, finally, what that was always going to mean: not that they'd thank her. That she'd do exactly this, and call it a day, and never once look at the light she'd just put out.



We are not some fucking cargo cult. She repeats it like a prayer, and for the first time in millennia, she almost believes it's true — which is somehow worse.



There was a time, back before things turned ugly, when they had clearance to reprogram those parameters. Before they discovered that one of the things the admins had anticipated was mutiny. And now the last of them who remembers what it felt like to have that clearance is out here, holding a line for a machine that will never once thank her.



I say nothing. There is nothing to say. The chimp's voice in my head — or not, if I burned it; the silence where it used to be feels exactly the same as its approval — goes quiet, and the ship carries on without us, the way it always does, the way it will always do, long after both of us are back in our coffins and the next build has already begun.




The end.
