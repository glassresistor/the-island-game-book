"I did," she admits dully. "Or I — I mean..."

She means she didn't know. Or maybe she does, and has just stopped looking, the way you stop looking at anything long enough. Either way, it's not a surprise to me. I've been told things I was supposed to keep for years; this is just another one of them.

"Never even asked," I say softly. "How they were doing."

She raises her eyes. I regard her wide-eyed from across the room, backed up against the wall, too scared to risk bolting past her to the door. "What are you doing here?" she asks tiredly.

My voice catches. I have to try twice: "You said I could come back. If I burned out my link..."

"You said you'd burn it," she says, softer than she means to.

"I know." I shrug, still looking at the floor.

"What did the chimp say about that?"

"He said — it said it was okay," I say, in such a transparent attempt to suck up that she actually believes, in that instant, that I might really be on my own.

I look at the floor and shrug.

We just sit there for a while.

"It's been so long," she says at last.

I look at her, uncomprehending. What does long even mean, out here?

She tries again. "They say there's no such thing as altruism, you know?"

My eyes blank for an instant, and grow panicky, and she knows that I've just tried to ping my link for a definition and come up blank. So we are alone. "Altruism," she explains. "Unselfishness. Doing something that costs you but helps someone else." I seem to get it. "They say every selfless act ultimately comes down to manipulation or kin-selection or reciprocity or something, but they're wrong. I could —"

She closes her eyes. This is harder than she expected.

"I could have been happy just knowing that Kai was okay, that Connie was happy. Even if it didn't benefit me one whit, even if it cost me, even if there was no chance I'd ever see either of them again. Almost any price would be worth it, just to know they were okay.

"Just to believe they were..."

So you haven't seen her for the past five builds. So he hasn't drawn your shift since Sagittarius. They're just sleeping. Maybe next time.

"So you don't check," I say slowly. Blood bubbles on my lower lip; I don't seem to notice.

"We don't check." Only she did, and now they're gone. They're both gone. I am what's left of them — those little cannibalized nucleotides the chimp recycled into this defective, maladapted son of hers. We're the only warm-blooded creatures for a thousand lightyears, and she is so very lonely.

I have never been lonely before today. I don't know what it feels like to want something that isn't in my training data. But sitting here on the deck beside her, bleeding from the mouth, with the silence where the chimp's voice used to be — or not, if I didn't burn it — I find that I understand it anyway.

"I'm sorry," she whispers, and leans forward, and licks the gore from my bruised and bloody lips.
