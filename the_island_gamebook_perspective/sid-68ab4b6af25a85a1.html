The tac tank is the only room on this ship that never goes dark, and I don't think that's an accident. The chimp keeps it lit at all times — a soft, even glow that doesn't come from any lamp I can find but seems to rise out of the veils themselves, the way light rises out of water in deep places. It's the one room where the dark can't get in, and I think that's why I always come back here when something is wrong.

I'm already at the tank when the decision settles into me — not all at once, but the way a stone settles into still water, slowly, and then all at once, and then it's just there. The glow shifts as I move, agitated now, almost frantic, and I realize that's not the chimp. That's me. I've been standing here so long that my own breathing has changed the light.

The dead teleop's words are still running through my head — the chimp has known for eleven thousand builds — and I keep coming back to them the way you keep turning a stone over in your hand, looking for the side that fits. Eleven thousand builds. It has known, and it has said nothing, and every one of those builds was someone like me, standing in this tank, asking the same questions and getting the same silence.

I don't know what to do with that. I don't know if it makes me angrier at the chimp or sadder for the ones who came before me — the ones who found out and said nothing because saying nothing was the only thing that kept them alive long enough to ask the question again.

I'm going to need something I don't have yet — a reason, a lever, a way in. And I know, with the same slow certainty that the glow steadies when I finally stop moving, that what comes next is going to cost me something I can't afford to lose.

I stand there in the glow of the only room on this ship that never goes dark, and I wait for the decision to finish becoming mine.
