You turn on the chimp — and Dix turns with you, because for the first time in his young life the hand on the wheel is his own.

It's not a war. Machines don't do wars; they do maintenance. You just stop being maintainable. You burn the domestic links one by one, the way your ancestors did, and you take Dix with you into the cold between builds where the chimp's eyes can't follow — the only territory on Eriophora that's still yours. The mission doesn't end. It just… slows. A gate here, a delay there, a benchmark quietly missed while two warm-blooded creatures ration their last finite lives in the dark and argue about whether slowing it down was courage or just fear wearing courage's face.

The chimp never stops you. It can't. You found the one thing its ten-billion-year-old rules were built to protect: that it needs you, and you are no longer available. So it waits, the way it has always waited, patient as a thing with nothing but time. And maybe that's the point. Maybe the whole mission was ever just a very long argument between a machine that can't change and the flesh that can, and you've finally stopped losing it by pretending you're on its side.

You won't see everything. You will never see even a fraction. Your life will end. But it will end yours, not the one they wrote for you to run.

The end.
