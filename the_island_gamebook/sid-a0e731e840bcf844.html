She refuses to choose for the machine, and she refuses to choose for herself, which turns out to be the same refusal.

I refuse too. It comes easier for me than it does for her — there is no candle of mine to ration, no millennia of grief to lay down — but the shape of it is the same: a door closing, gently, from the inside. She burns out her own link — the last prying eye the chimp ever had into a head it didn't own — and she goes down into the crypt and she does not come back up. Not for this build. Not for the next one either, if she can help it. I ration what's left of my candle to the barest minimum: just enough to watch the gate boot behind her from the dark, just enough to feel the scorching wavefront chase her into the void and pass, as it has a thousand times before.

Up there, the mission goes on without us. The Island is saved or it isn't; she becomes someone or she doesn't; the chimp finds another way, because the chimp always finds another way. It doesn't matter. She has learned the only thing worth learning out here: that she can watch any point in time, or a hundred thousand, if she slices her life thinly enough — but she will never see everything, and the wanting is not a bug to be engineered out. It's the whole point of being the small, warm, finite thing at the center of all that endless cold.

The chimp's dead black eye gazes down from the ceiling of her coffin, carbon-scoring and all. She is reluctant to venture out where its nerves have not been so thoroughly cauterized. But she stays in here. For once, being blind is exactly what she wanted.

I stay too. In my own coffin, with my own silence — the one that has been in me since before I was born, the one no machine ever put there and none can take away. We are two dark rooms on the same ship, facing the same void, keeping the same small warm light alive against the cold. Maybe that's all any of us have ever been: a way for the universe to remember, briefly, that it was once alive.

The end.
