You go to the library to find the weapon in it, and you tell yourself that's all. You start again from first principles, because that's the only honest way to look at a god you can't yet name.

You learn the alchemy by which photosynthesis turns light and gas and electrons into living tissue. You calculate lower metabolic limits for a life-form that filters organics from the ether. You marvel at the speed of its thoughts — almost as fast as Eri flies, orders of magnitude faster than any mammalian nerve impulse. Some kind of organic superconductor, passing chilled electrons almost resistance-free out here in the freezing void.

And then it comes to you, the way these things do: violence is a planetary phenomenon.

Planets are the abusive parents of evolution. Their surfaces concentrate resources into dense defensible patches that can be fought over. Gravity forces you to squander energy on vascular systems and skeletal support, to stand endless watch against a sadistic campaign to squash you flat. Take one wrong step off a perch too high and all your pricey architecture shatters. Is it any wonder you grew up believing life was a struggle, that zero-sum was God's own law, that the future belonged to those who crushed the competition?

The rules are so different out here. Most of space is tranquil: no diel cycles, no ice ages, no pendulum swings between hot and cold. Molecular clouds glow with organic chemistry and life-giving radiation. This Island puts the lie to everything you were ever told about the machinery of life. Sun-powered, perfectly adapted, immortal — it won no struggle for survival. Where are the predators, the competitors, the parasites? All of life around 428 is one vast continuum, one grand act of symbiosis. Nature out here is not red in tooth and claw. Nature out here is the helping hand.

Lacking the capacity for violence, the Island has outlasted worlds. Unencumbered by technology, it has out-thought civilizations. It is intelligent beyond your measure — and, you're growing sure, benign. How can it even conceive of an enemy?
