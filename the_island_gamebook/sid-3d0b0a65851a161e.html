You move the gate. You moved it exactly as far as the Island told you to, and you tell yourself that's all it ever was — a request, from a being so vast and so old that asking is the only verb it knows.

We do not stop. There is no force in the universe that can even slow you down. Past equals present; Eriophora dives through the center of the gate in a nanosecond. The unimaginable mass of her cold black heart snags some distant dimension, drags it screaming to the here and now. The booted portal erupts behind you, blossoms into a great blinding corona, every wavelength lethal to every living thing. Our aft filters clamp down tight.

An hour doesn't pass when you don't check the numbers again. They were right. The Island just had — enemies. Victims, anyway. You were right about one thing: that fucker was smart. To see you coming, to figure out how to talk to you; to use you as a weapon, to turn a threat to its very existence into a…

You guess flyswatter is as good a word as any.

"Maybe there was a war," you mumble. "Maybe it wanted the real estate. Or maybe it was just some — family squabble."

"Maybe didn't know," Dix suggests, in that careful way that means he's not inventing these rosy alternatives for himself. He's trying to comfort you. You don't need to be coddled. You were a fool: you let yourself believe in life without conflict, in sentience without sin. For a little while you dwelt in a dream world where every living thing did not struggle to exist at the expense of other life. You deified that which you could not understand, when in the end it was all too easily understood.

But you're better now. It's over: another build, another benchmark, another irreplaceable slice of life that brings your task no closer to completion. Mission accomplished is a meaningless phrase on Eriophora, an ironic oxymoron at best. There may one day be failure, but there is no finish line. You go on forever, crawling across the universe like ants, dragging your goddamned superhighway behind us.

I wonder if you'll notice the corpse we left behind.

The end.
