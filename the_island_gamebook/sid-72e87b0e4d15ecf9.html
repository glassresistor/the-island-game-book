"They say there's no such thing as altruism," you explain. "Unselfishness. Doing something that costs you but helps someone else. They say every selfless act comes down to manipulation or kin-selection or reciprocity. But they're wrong. I could have been happy just knowing that Kai was okay, that Connie was happy. Even if it didn't benefit me one whit. Almost any price would have been worth it, just to believe they were…"

"So you don't check," Dix says slowly. Blood bubbles on his lower lip; he doesn't seem to notice.

"We don't check." Only you did. And now they're gone. You mean: three builds back. One airlock jammed, the next too far away along Eri's hull, a hail-Mary emergency crawl between. They made it back inside but not before the blue-shifted background cooked them in their suits. They kept breathing for hours afterwards, talked and moved and cried as if they were still alive, while their insides broke down and bled out.

There were two others awake that shift. Ishmael, and — you. You didn't know. Or you did, the way you always do: you just stopped looking, after a while.

"Never even asked," your son says softly, "how they were doing."

You raise your eyes. He regards you wide-eyed from across the room, backed against the wall, too scared to risk bolting past you to the door. "What are you doing here?" you ask tiredly. His voice catches. He has to try twice: "You said I could come back. If I burned out my link…" You realize he's done it. He's actually done it. For the first time in his young life, the words coming out of that lopsided mouth are only his own.

"I'm sorry," you whisper, and lean forward, and lick the gore from his bruised and bloody lips.
